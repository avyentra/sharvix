A Practical Cloud Migration Checklist

Good migrations are mostly planning. Use this checklist before you move anything.

Avyentra TeamNovember 3, 20262 min read

In this article

  2. Map the dependencies
  3. Choose an approach for each system
  4. Protect your data
  5. Test, then cut over
  6. Watch closely afterwards
  7. Mistakes that cause delays
  8. Quick checklist

Moving to the cloud can improve reliability and make it easier to scale, but a rushed move creates new problems. Most of the risk can be reduced with careful planning before anything is moved.

Take stock of what you have

Start with a list of your applications, databases, integrations and scheduled jobs. Note who owns each one and how important it is to the business.

Many surprises come from small background services that nobody remembered.

Map the dependencies

Find out what talks to what. If a billing service relies on an old database, those two may need to move together. A simple diagram can prevent hours of confusion later.

Choose an approach for each system

Some systems can be moved largely as they are. Others deserve small changes to use managed services, and a few may be better replaced. You do not need one answer for everything.

Protect your data

Take tested backups before migration, and confirm that you can restore them. Plan access controls and encryption from the start, not after the move.

Test, then cut over

Run a trial migration and test performance, security and integrations. Schedule the final move for a quiet period, and write down a rollback plan.

Watch closely afterwards

Monitor errors, performance and cost after launch. Cloud spending can grow quietly, so set budgets and alerts early.

Mistakes that cause delays

Teams often underestimate how long data transfer takes, especially for large databases. Test the transfer early so that your schedule is based on real numbers.

Another common problem is moving everything at once. Migrating in smaller stages, starting with a lower-risk system, gives your team practice and limits the impact if something goes wrong.

Remember your people as well. Make sure the team knows how to deploy, monitor and support the new setup, and update your documentation before the old environment is switched off.

Quick checklist

  • List every system and owner
  • Map dependencies
  • Test a backup restore
  • Run a trial migration
  • Write a rollback plan
  • Set up monitoring and cost alerts
